A Hamiltonian system is naturally represented in phase space by first-order equations generated by a Hamiltonian and a symplectic structure.

## Mathematical skeleton

In canonical coordinates q-dot equals partial H by partial p and p-dot equals minus partial H by partial q.

The claim is scoped to Finite-dimensional smooth Hamiltonian dynamics. Its stated validity regime is: Canonical coordinates locally, symplectic vector fields globally.

## Boundaries

- Dissipative systems are not Hamiltonian without extension or reformulation.

Counterexamples and failure probes:

- A generic contracting flow does not preserve symplectic volume.

## Adversarial review

**Challenge.** Merely conserving an energy-like scalar does not make a system Hamiltonian.

**Response.** The definition requires the symplectic generation relation, not conservation alone.
